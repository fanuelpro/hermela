Featured Mission — Mental Health Advocacy

The Film That Almost Didn't Get Made.

UNMUTE

Long before Gergesenon — before any camera or competition category — she was already drawn to the parts of being human that don't announce themselves: the battles no one applauds, the stories people hide behind a smile. The loss of a 38-year-old relative to suicide shattered the illusion that mental health only affects the individual struggling. It left her with a haunting question: how many more families must lose someone they love before we treat psychological well-being with the urgency it deserves? When it came time to turn that instinct into an advocacy film for the Gergesenon Association's mental health initiative, she froze at the very first step.

"Awareness isn't the accumulation of answers. It's the willingness to let reality replace certainty."

What followed was research most competitors skip entirely — conversations with experts, organizations, researchers, volunteers, and people with lived experience, each one dismantling an assumption she didn't know she held. Sleepless nights, endless rewrites, hours on the road, and more than once, an hour of sleep on an office bench before a full day of filming.

"The camera documented an advocacy video. The process documented a transformation."

The finished film is public. The commitment behind it isn't finished at all — Gergesenon, she says, taught her that healing is never an individual achievement. It's built by communities willing to keep showing up for one another.

"This competition gave me a platform, but it didn't give me my purpose. It simply amplified one that was already growing quietly within me."

Ambassadorship — African Union

A Seat at the Continental Table.

As part of her ambassadorial responsibilities, she is developing engagement with African Union initiatives focused on women's economic and educational empowerment — advocating for policy conversations that too often happen without the women they're meant to serve in the room.

  • ✦ Continental forums on women's economic participation
  • ✦ Youth delegate programming alongside AU gender initiatives
  • ✦ Cross-border advocacy tied to her Miss Tourism Africa platform

Ambassadorship — STEM Empowerment

Code as a Second Native Tongue.

Drawing directly from her own path through a computer science degree, into a career most Ethiopian girls are never shown as possible, she is building "Girls Who Build" — a hands-on STEM and engineering exposure initiative for secondary school girls across Ethiopia, using her own trajectory as the proof of concept.

  • ✦ Hands-on coding and engineering workshops in partner schools
  • ✦ Mentorship pairing university CS students with secondary schoolers
  • ✦ A public case study: her own path, documented step by step
