# PHOTOGRAPHY REFERENCE BRIEF — HERMELA ASHENAFI ASSEFA
### For handover to a photographer / creative director ahead of the real shoot
Companion to `blueprint.md`. These are **style and genre references only** — mood, light,
silhouette, and texture — never images to be copied, never tied to a specific celebrity
shoot or magazine issue. Nothing here should be licensed or reproduced; it exists to get
a photographer speaking the same visual language as the site before the real shoot happens.

---

## 1. Sovereign Structure — the power silhouette
Her own answer to 2.1: bold, architectural, commanding. Reference for tailoring, shoulder
line, and how a garment should hold a room.
- Look for: structured blazers and gowns with real shoulder architecture, not soft drape.
- Light: hard-ish key light that lets the garment's edges read as clean lines, not silhouette-flattening soft light.
- Avoid: anything soft-focus or romantic — this category is about authority, not prettiness.

## 2. Fluid & Kinetic — the counterweight
Her answer to 2.2(C): "a curated juxtaposition of both structural and fluid elements."
This is the other half — never the whole shoot.
- Look for: silk, crepe, and fine linen captured mid-movement — fabric caught in motion, not styled static.
- Best used sparingly: one or two frames per look, contrasted against a structured piece elsewhere in the same story.

## 3. Elevated High-Key — the actual chosen environment
Her real answer to 3.1 (not the brief's "Abyssal Deep" — see blueprint.md §0). This is the
base lighting environment for nearly everything on the site.
- Look for: wide, open, bright sets — optical off-whites, warm alabaster walls, raw linen backdrops.
- Light: soft, large, high-key — skin and fabric both read luminous, never blown out or flat.
- This should be roughly 80% of the shoot's lighting mood. The remaining 20% (see §6) is where "Abyssal Deep" survives, deliberately, as contrast.

## 4. Architectural Minimalism — environment and set
For location and set-design reference across Identity, Reign, and Codes pages.
- Look for: raw concrete, warm brutalist forms, strong single-source natural light through large openings.
- Use as backdrop, not subject — she is always the sharpest thing in the frame.

## 5. Tailored Power Suit — Identity & Reign pages
For the project-manager / strategic-innovation side of her — CS graduate, leader, not just model.
- Look for: sculptural tailoring, structured trousers or gowns, a posture of command rather than a fashion pose.
- Note: several search results in this category surfaced celebrity red-carpet photography and AI-generated "Vogue cover" tools — neither is a usable or appropriate reference; the useful signal here is silhouette and tailoring only, not any specific person's shoot.

## 6. Regal / Crown — Reign page exclusively
This register (Awe & Authority) is intentionally concentrated on one page only, per blueprint.md §5.
- Look for: sculptural crown or headpiece work, strong single-light regal portraiture.
- This is the one place "Abyssal Deep" — deep shadow, dramatic single-source light — is the right call, precisely because it's rare elsewhere on the site.

## 7. Movement & Ritual — Identity & Codes pages (Morning Code)
For the "I dance to reconnect with myself" ritual moment.
- Look for: a dancer mid-movement in a sunlit studio, natural unposed energy, motion blur on limbs while the face stays legible.
- This is the most human, least "campaign" moment on the site — light should feel like real morning sun, not a studio strobe.

## 8. Texture & Tone — supporting detail shots
For the Portfolio page's tactility section and any macro/detail cutaways.
- Look for: close crops of bronze/umber/warm-mineral tones in fabric or material — the accent color family made tactile.
- These are connective tissue shots, not headline images — use between two larger portraits, never as an opener.

## Shot List Priorities (in order of what the site actually needs first)
1. One hero portrait, Elevated High-Key, full-length or three-quarter, Sovereign Structure garment — **this replaces the Unsplash placeholder on Home and Portfolio.**
2. Five to seven lookbook frames alternating Sovereign Structure and Fluid & Kinetic, for the Portfolio pinned gallery.
3. One Regal/Crown portrait, deliberately darker and more dramatic than everything else — for Reign.
4. One candid movement frame for the Morning Code section on Identity/Codes.
5. Two to three architectural/environment plates with no figure, for background parallax layers — these can be shot fast, wide, and are the lowest priority of the set.
